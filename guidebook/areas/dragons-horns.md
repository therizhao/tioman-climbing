# The Dragon's Horns (Bukit / Gunung Nenek Semukut)

> Source: PDF pages 28–75.

The **Dragon's Horns** are the twin granite towers of **Gunung Nenek Semukut**, rising
above **Kampung Mukut** on Tioman's south coast — feature **H** on the
[overview map](../00-overview.md) and the reason climbers come to the island. The
**South Tower** (higher, on the left/west) holds most of the routes; the **North Tower**
(right/east) has one.

## Approach & descent (general)

- All South Tower routes start from the **Nenek Semukut hiking trail out of Mukut**
  (from **Simukut Hillview / Tanjung Inn**), following numbered checkpoints:
  - **CP5** — branch for **Batu Naga** and **Damai Sentosa** (south / south-west side).
    Between two massive boulders one trail goes right to CP6, the other goes up-left toward
    the South Tower.
  - **CP7** — branch (turn **left / uphill**) for **Polish Princess**, **Sam Sam**,
    **Naga**, **Freebird**, and **Puncak Anak**. ~10 min above CP7 for Polish Princess.
  - **CP9** — on the far side; the **Naga** line is visible from here.
- Approaches range **~1 h (Polish Princess) to 2 h+ (Damai Sentosa, "quite hard")**.
- **Descents are long committing abseils** down the line (Polish Princess, Damai Sentosa,
  Sam Sam, Waking Dream) — frequently finished after dark. Damai Sentosa is *"2 hours of
  straightforward abseiling"*. Several parties recommend **70 m ropes**.
- Some routes finish with **"jungle climbing"** pitches (grade ~IV / 5b–5c) and a walk along
  the **summit ridge trail** to the true summit (**"The Wart"** on the South Tower).

### The "Dragon Tour" traverse

Source page 30 is a logbook page for a **2-day / 1-night trek-and-traverse** of the massif
(*"trek-making of the new route, 22–24 May 2019"*), with a base camp + water near **CP4/CP5**
and a route over the **saddle** and **viewpoint** between the towers. Notes warn against
doing the scramble section at the start, flag **serious fall danger**, and suggest a
**hammock rather than a tent**, and taking **a guide**.

### Batu Naga ("Stone Dragon")

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | 5.12a (**5.12R** on page 31) |
| **Length** | 300 m, **8 pitches**, IV |
| **First ascent** | **Cedar Wright & Lucho Rivera, 2011** |
| **Rock / aspect** | *"South-facing white buttress"* |
| **Rack** | Single set of cams to **#3 Camalot**, extra thin, **lots of slings (10+)**, 1 set stoppers, 6 quickdraws, **2 ropes**. *"Be prepared for long runouts on easier ground, on bullet knobs and flakes!! Hand drill???"* |

**Approach (verbatim from source page 45):**
> *"Approach from Mukut to Dragons Horns trekking trail to Checkpoint 5. This is in between
> 2 massive boulders. One trail goes right towards Checkpoint 6 and one goes up and left
> towards the South Dragons Horn. Take the left trail up to the base of the wall. Continue
> left when the wall is reached along the base staying high. A deep gully is crossed high
> along spikey ferns, then eventually obvious black streaks can be seen on a white wall just
> above. Continue left for 100+ feet to an old basecamp with water jug and lantern. Here head
> straight up old fixed ropes, 3rd and 4th class. Scramble up a small step out right to reach
> the base of the first pitch."*

**Descent (verbatim):**
> *"To summit from last pitch scramble left from last anchor then immediately right and up a
> gully to a brushy ledge. Go right across boulders to a rock groove gully then head right
> until the final step can be seen up and slightly left. Head straight up to summit ridge
> trail."*

The hand-drawn topo (source page 45) marks pitch grades around **5.11− / 5.11− / 5.12
("dynamic crux") / 5.11+ ("technical crimps") / 5.11** on the lower half.

### Damai Sentosa

*"Great steep climb on compact rock (almost all bolted)."* The most-repeated hard route on
the tower, and the one that has seen the most **anchor upgrading** (titanium / stainless
glue-ins added by David Acott and others).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | **6c+** (6b oblig; **8a variation**). Page 38: *"6c+ or 6b+ / A1"* |
| **Length** | 280 m, ~8 pitches, **SW Pillar of Nenek Semukut** |
| **First ascent** | **Stéphanie Bodet, David Kaszlikowski, Dan Liu (Liu Yong), Arnaud Petit, Tam Khairudin Haja — April 2013** |
| **Gear** | **60 m ropes** (50 m should be OK). Max 10 bolts per pitch. Cams: small selection **0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 1, 2**. P1 right start: take all; P1 left start (in the tree): none. P2: take all. P3: #2 cam. Higher: nothing until the last pitch (small cam) |
| **Conditions** | *"On the shade before 1 pm in late April."* |
| **Descent** | Abseil the line — *"2 hours of straightforward abseiling"* |

**Approach (verbatim from source page 46):**
> *"Quite hard. From Tanjung take the Simukut hill trail. At CP5 follow the trail on left,
> after 10' this trail starts to be not obvious; you reach the wall, then go left and follow
> the base staying 50–60 m lower to go up again and reach the S-W pillar. 1h to 2h depending
> [on] the quality of the trail."*

**Pitch list — from the hand-drawn topos (source pages 46–47), approximate:**

| P | Grade | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6b+ (tree-climb start) / 6a+ variation | 30 m | Right start harder; left start via the tree |
| 2 | 6c | 25 m | |
| 3 | 6c+ | 45 m | *"8a?"* variation branches here |
| 4 | 6c+ | 45 m | mental crux — *"big runouts but easy climbing"* |
| 5 | 6c | ~30 m | crux well bolted |
| 6 | 6b+ | 50 m | |
| 7 | 6b | 40–50 m | *"harder than the 6b+ pitch 6 before"* — the *"sketchy 7th pitch"* |
| 8 | 6b | 40 m | to the summit slabs |

**Trip-report highlights:**
- **Lee Neale & Kate Sabo, 9 July 2015** (source page 48): 2 h approach; on trail by 5 am;
  summited 5 pm; *"Pitch 2–3 are wandery, Pitch 4 is the mental crux — big runouts but easy
  climbing. Cruxes on pitch 3 & 5 are very well bolted. Pitch 7 is harder than the 6b+ pitch
  6 before. Lots of slings … small offsets very useful."* Thanks Little Planet (Juara) for
  kayaks to Mukut.
- **Mike Söldner & Nicolas, 21 April 2018** (source page 49): *"A perfect weekend adventure
  escape from city life in Singapore. … Perfect rock, very safe bolting, only one major
  shower on route, a well-marked trail meant we reached the top by 5 pm. 2 hours of
  straightforward abseiling saw us back at the base at 10 pm and back at Simukut at midnight.
  … Thanks to the visionary first ascentionists, the guys doing the re-bolting, and everyone
  maintaining the trail."*

### Sam Sam

The **2010 Kaszlikowski / Kubarska line** — *"currently the fastest free line to the top of
the South Tower."* Later used as the **approach for Polish Princess**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | **7b** (page 38) / *"6c max"* (topo) |
| **Length** | ~300 m (topo says *"ca. 300 m"*, also *"220 m"* of climbing) |
| **First ascent** | **Eliza Kubarska & David Kaszlikowski, 21–22 August 2010** — 2 days, bivouac on top |
| **Descent** | Abseil; **Czech rap anchors** marked on the topo |

**From the topo (source page 57):** starts from the jungle/path, `VI` and *"6c overhang,
loose stone"* low down, `R2` 6b+, then belays R3–R8 through a **"black roof"** and a `60 m`
pitch to the top. *"Jungle part [near the top] not very pleasant — go as [high] as possible,
no anchors installed."* Contact given as `david@verticalvision.pl`.

### Polish Princess

*"★★★★★ route!!!"* — the recommended **direct free line to the South Tower summit**, fully
equipped for repeats. Uses **Sam Sam as its approach** and shares a belay (R3) with the
2010 Czech route (crossed by accident during the first ascent).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | **7b+** (7b max; topo pitch grades below) |
| **Length** | 260–270 m, **8 pitches** to *"Dragon's Horns TOP"* |
| **First ascent** | **Eliza Kubarska & David Kaszlikowski — 13 April 2011** (redpoint) |
| **Gear** | Full rack of cams (**to BD #4**) + some medium nuts, **60 m rope** (a 70 m works for the rappels) |
| **Approach** | From **Tanjung Inn up to CP7**, then left / uphill ~10 min |
| **Descent** | Abseil the line — rappel stances in place. **1 × 70 m rope OK** (60 m for some pitches). *Beware: parties have been benighted here in cloud.* |

| 2 | 6c | 30 m | *Watch a big separated boulder ~4 m above the belay* |
| 3 | 6b | 35 m | *Run-out at a slab but easy — place some extra pro ~2 m above the second bolt.* Common belay with the Czech route |
| 4 | 7a (6c+) | 40 m | *Great pillar — climb the right side of the edge.* A **6a/a+ variation** exists (page 60) |
| 5 | 6a | 25 m | |
| 6 | 6a+ | 35 m | |
| 7 | 6c | 25 m | *Great pillar, easy to protect* |
| 8 | jungle climbing IV | 25 m | *Climb the jungle to the top, nice boulder* |

- P1: 2 thaitanium bolt anchors
- P2: 1 SS 316 twisted anchor bolt + 2 thaitanium anchors
- P3: 1 SS 316 twisted anchor bolt + 1 thaitanium anchor + **1 drilled 14 mm hole**

### Naga ("The Dragon" / 龍)

The **2017 Malaysian + Chinese first ascent** — *"might be the first local bolted
multipitch route with a Chinese climber."* Straight line, mostly bolted face climbing with
cracks / trees left natural. Visible from **CP9** on the far side of the peak. Featured in
the YouTube film **"Scaling Dragons"** (source page 64).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | **6b+** (also given Chinese grade **C3**) |
| **Length** | 270 m, **8 pitches** |
| **First ascent** | **8 April 2017** — Fizi Ishak (MAS), Aril Hasril (MAS), Dorge @ Apek Langit Biru (MAS), Da Liu / Liu Yong (CHN) |
| **Second ascent** | Izzuddin Husin, Syazwan Hassan, Fikri Md Nawi |
| **Gear** | 1 set of cams (runout option without cams/nuts), **10 alpine draws**, 1 × 70 m or 2 × 50 m ropes, **2 L water per person**, some runners |
| **Approach** | From **Hillview Lodge** along the trail to **CP7**, then **turn left and keep hiking up** to the wall. Start at a tree marked **"BC"** (climb starts at a steep corner just left of it; first hanger ~5 m up) |
| **Base camp** | On the foot of the wall — hammock with fly recommended. FA's base camp marked **"DBC"**; Polish Princess base camp ("BC") is an alternative |

**Climbing notes (FA, source page 67):**
> *"Start from a small crack, go straight, first bolt is 5 m high, then climb straight up to
> the overhang part, then turn to the right to the first anchor. Second pitch is easy
> straight climbing to the right, then all the way straight up. We made good anchors on every
> pitch. **Beware of loose rock on pitches 3 and 4.** The last pitch is almost steep hiking
> along the trail to the big rock on the summit. We bolted most of the face-climbing parts
> and left the cracks, trees and some natural protection."*

**Second-ascent pitch notes (source page 68)** — the SA party **linked pitches** (2+3, 5+6)
into 6 pitches:
1. Follow the obvious crack left of the "BC" tree; after the 3rd bolt head right to the
   first anchor (they built one on the tree above-left instead).
2. Link the original P2+P3; anchor at the abseil point.
3. Follow the bolt line right of a dirty crack; past a tree blocking the crack to a stance
   above.
4. Link original P5+P6 into bushy ground with a big tree; anchor on the left.
5. **Crux pitch** — straight up into the face between the crack (left) and the black face
   (right); past the crux, committing **mantle** into a slope, long runout to the anchor
   up-right. All tree anchors.
6. Technical scramble, easy moves **5b–5c**, into the upper jungle; follow the trail to the
   summit.

### Freebird

A **slab / crack adventure route** on **Puncak Nenek Simukut** (the South Tower area,
reached from CP7 — near [Puncak Anak](puncak-anak.md)). *"Nice route with runouts in
beautiful cracks and slabs."* Popular with Malaysian teams; several recorded ascents and a
direct variation.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | 6a, **TD, IV** |
| **Length** | 230–250 m, ~7 pitches (R1–R7 + jungle to the top) |
| **First ascent** | **9–10 October 2015** — Timothée Guillon, Nicolas Gay, Apull Shaiful Amin, Tam Khairudin Haja |
| **Gear** | **2 racks of cams**, set of nuts, **2 × 50 m ropes**, lots of slings. *"Nothing on place."* |
| **Descent** | Abseil **left onto Polish Princess** |

**Pitch grades (FA topo, source page 72):** R1 6a, R2 5c, R3 5a, R4 5c, R5 6a, R6 6a,
R7 (tree/jungle) → jungle traverse to the top. Loose flake / loose rock warnings low down.

**Recorded ascents / variations:**
- **Freebird Direct** — **4 June 2016**, Izzu, Ridzuan, Raouf (source page 71). 6 pitches:
  6a / 5c / 5 / 5c / 6a / 6b. *"P2: use long slings — better belay stance on the lower left.
  P4: traverse exactly on the loose slab, head right. P5: direct into the dyno crack —
  slightly harder but better pro (~40 m)."* *"A nice route for slab aficionados."*
- **3rd ascent — 22 August 2016** ("Merdeka Trip 2016"), lead: Ibrahim Nasir & Mohd Ikhwan;
  support: Hazman Hariqan & Akmal Aliff (source page 70). Detailed pitch-by-pitch fixes to
  the FA topo (added nuts at R3, skipped R4, tree anchors).

## North Tower

### Tanoshi Buttress ("Happy Buttress")

The **only route on the North Tower** — first ascent of the North Dragon's Horn.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | **5.10 R/X** |
| **Length** | 270 m, **7 pitches**, IV |
| **First ascent** | **Cedar Wright & Lucho Rivera, 2011** |
| **Rack** | Double set of cams, one #3 Camalot, one #4 Camalot, set of stoppers, lots of runners, hand drill |

**From the logbook (source page 45):**
