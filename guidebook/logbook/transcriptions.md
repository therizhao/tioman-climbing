## Fever Dreams — Mumbar Cliff — David Kaszlikowski & Jonas Wallin
**Source page 9** · [`scans/page-09.jpg`](scans/page-09.jpg)

> **Expedition Mumbar (aka Kota Sirau).** Jonas Wallin & David Kaszlikowski.
> First ascent on the wall, first trail up the mountain — "Fever Dreams" route.
>
> In March/April 2016 we spent 3 quite exhausting weeks making another vertical vision
> come true. Since the mountain Mumbar was never climbed before, we needed to find and
> cut the approach trail first. Luckily, with help from Uncle Sam and his team, and after
> some planning (with [?] aerial photos in use) we managed to reach the mountain just in
> 3 hours (27.03.2016). Team that day was special: Uncle Sam himself, Tam, Muar
> volunteers, Serena and us — Jonas and David.
>
> Another important step was to find the start of the climbing route, which is typically
> a daunting task here in the jungles of Tioman. We spent 2 days before finally managing
> to emerge above the tree line.
>
> Our climb was above us — white stripe of steep granite, located just in the right from
> the arête dividing the mountain into 2 sectors: left with lower-angle black/brown slabs
> (great clean rock there) and steeper right wall.
>
> Since we had problems with transport (best option is boat to the "enter" beach and ask
> for a return boat to come for you at Tunamaya jetty), we spent many days walking up/down
> (10 km) which finished with cramps, fever and dehydration.
>
> It is my fourth route in the massif, and I have to say I'm glad that because of the
> quality of climbs and after media publications, Dragon Horns slowly turns into a regular
> "world" climbing destination. 🙂
>
> — David Kaszlikowski

## Damai Sentosa — Lee Neale & Kate Sabo — summited 9 July 2015
**Source page 48** · [`scans/page-48.jpg`](scans/page-48.jpg)

> **Damai Sentosa** — July 2015 — Tioman ♥
>
> First, big thanks to Tam, Uncle Sam and Epple (Apple?) [and food!] for your kindness,
> hospitality and help! Would have never been able to find the start of the route if it
> wasn't for Epple [and Stephan!] showing us the approach the day before. Also big thanks
> to David Acott for recently adding thaitanium and SS glue-ins to rap stations and the
> sketchy 7th pitch! (Phew!)
>
> What an adventure! We stashed all our gear the day before so our packs were light for
> the 2 hr approach. Woke up at 4:15 am and on the trail by 5, at the top of the tree
> pitch by 7 am. Our goal was to finish the crux pitches before the sun hit at 1 pm. We
> summited at 5 pm and rapped as the sun set…
>
> Pitch 2–3 are wandery, Pitch 4 is the mental crux… big runouts but easy climbing. The
> cruxes on pitch 3 & 5 are very well bolted. Pitch 7 [wet] is harder than the 6b+ pitch 6
> before. Lots of slings to sling natural pro — very very helpful. Lots of nut placements
> when the runouts got spicy. We also brought up a small set of off-sets which proved to
> be very useful. Be prepared for hard-man bolting and creative pro!
>
> Summited 9th of July 2015. Lee Neale (royalneale@gmail.com) and Kate Sabo
> (kzoesabo@gmail.com). Thanks to Little Planet in Juara for lending us kayaks to get to
> Mukut! xx
>
> GREAT CLIMB! BEAUTIFUL VIEWS! "Get yo' crimp on!"

## Naga ("The Dragon" / 龍) — first ascent — 8 April 2017
**Source page 67** · [`scans/page-67.jpg`](scans/page-67.jpg)

> **"The Dragon" → "Naga" 龍** — 270 m, 6B+, 8 pitches
>
> First Ascent: Fizi Ishak, Aril Hasril, Dorge @ Apek Langit Biru, Da Liu (Liu Yong),
> 08 April 2017.
>
> This might be the first local bolted multi-pitch route with a Chinese climber. It has a
> very nice view and straight line. The climbing line is visible from CP9 from the other
> side of the peak.
>
> **Trail:** start hike from Hillview Lodge along the trail till CP7, then turn to the
> left, keep hiking up to the wall.
>
> **Base camp:** we built a nice base camp on the foot of the wall in case of raining or
> bivy in the jungle.
>
> **Climbing:** start from the small crack, go straight, first bolt is 5 m high, then
> climb straight up to the overhang part, then turn to the right to first anchor. Second
> pitch is easy straight climbing to the right, then all the way straight up. We made good
> anchors on every pitch. Beware of some loose rock on pitch 3 and 4. The last is almost
> steep hiking along the trail, leads to the big rock on the summit. We bolted most of the
> face-climbing parts and left the crack, trees and some of natural protection.
>
> **Gear:** 1 set of cams, 1 × 70 m rope or 2 × 50 m ropes, some runners, bring some water.
>
> Don't forget give good comments for our hand works. Special thanks Abe's help & members
> of CMC. Work: MAS Climbers & Chinese Mountain Club (华岳会).
>
> — Da Liu (viaphupha@gmail.com), April 08 2017

## Freebird Direct — Izzu, Ridzuan, Raouf — 4 June 2016
**Source page 71** · [`scans/page-71.jpg`](scans/page-71.jpg)

> **Freebird Direct** — 4th June 2016 — Izzu, Ridzuan, Raouf
>
> **1st pitch (6a):** Nice slab with 50–50 protection. Sketchy move about 8 [m] from
> ground. Easy traverse to the anchor.
> **2nd pitch (5c):** We go up straight into the right overhang section and traverse left.
> ⚠ USE LONG SLINGS. There's a better belay stance on the lower left.
> **3rd pitch (5):** Easy section with pockets / slabby roots and flaring crack after
> minimal pro. Creative placements 🙂
> **4th pitch (5c):** TRAVERSE EXACTLY ON THE LOOSE SLAB. Head towards the right.
> **5th pitch (6a):** We decided to go direct into the dyno crack, slightly more
> challenging but better pro. Around 40 m.
> **6th pitch (6b):** going up direct on the line above R5 straight up to summit. Around
> 20 m.
>
> Overall, a nice route for slab aficionados along with beautiful rock quality and OK
> runouts. Special thanks to Pak Tam and Uncle Sam for the place and food. Credit to
> B.V. [?], Vertical Adventures and Equipments and those who made it possible.
>
> — Izzu &nbsp; #kembaraboldervampires #tiomanclimber
